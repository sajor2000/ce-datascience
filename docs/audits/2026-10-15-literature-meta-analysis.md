# Audit: conventional healthcare systematic reviews and meta-analysis

**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Scope:** `ce-datascience` support for a PhD-level conventional healthcare systematic review/meta-analysis, including R and Python. This is a methods audit, not a claim that a reporting checklist establishes methods validity.

## Bottom line

The plugin has useful building blocks: current PRISMA routing, a registry-package draft, literature/method extraction, and an R `meta::metagen()` helper that uses REML and emits a prediction interval. It is **not yet safe to represent as an end-to-end conventional systematic-review/meta-analysis workflow**. The existing `ce-effect-size` skill says it is an effect-size anchor for power, and explicitly excludes full systematic-review meta-analysis. That boundary is correct and should remain until the gaps below are closed.

The main risk is not the absence of a forest plot. It is pooling a set of per-paper estimates without a protocol-level estimand, result-level risk-of-bias assessment, an independent-effect plan, and a missing-evidence/certainty assessment. PRISMA, MOOSE, and SWiM improve reporting transparency. They do not validate whether those methods are appropriate.

## Current external baseline

- **PRISMA 2020** is a reporting guideline for systematic reviews, with extensions for review types and aspects. Use its current official checklist, flow diagram, and relevant extension, but do not use completion as a methods-quality score. [PRISMA](https://www.prisma-statement.org/) and [official extensions index](https://www.prisma-statement.org/extensions)
- **MOOSE** remains the reporting guideline for meta-analyses of observational studies. It is complementary to PRISMA, not a replacement for confounding, selection-bias, or causal-estimand decisions. [MOOSE](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/192614)
- **Cochrane Handbook:** use the live current handbook and the chapter's own update date at implementation, rather than hard-coding a version in skill prose. Directly retrieved Chapter 10 supports explicit random-effects assumptions, prediction intervals, and caution with small study counts; Chapter 13 supports guarded funnel-asymmetry assessment. A search result for the official version-history page reported a 2026 update and ROB-ME, but that version claim was not independently retrievable in a stable primary page during this audit, so it is not treated as verified here. [Handbook current landing page](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current), [Chapter 10](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-10), and [Chapter 13](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-13).
- **PROSPERO:** current eligibility and timing policy could not be verified in this audit. The live registry rendered only a JavaScript-required shell in the available reader, and the independent Browserbase remote session could not start. The accessible wording that excludes reviews after completed data extraction was found in a 2023 registered protocol and is historical evidence, not current policy. Do not encode a timing threshold in a skill. Require a live official eligibility/timing check, record the accessed guidance URL/date, and use an OSF/protocol fallback when the review is ineligible or the registry does not accept its review type. [PROSPERO live registry](https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/) and [current guidance URL requiring live verification](https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/documents/Registering%20a%20review%20on%20PROSPERO.pdf).
- **RoB 2:** current individual, parallel-group tool is **22 August 2019**. Cluster and crossover variants were revised **18 March 2021**. It assesses bias for a specific result, not a trial-level quality score. [RoB 2 current version](https://www.riskofbias.info/welcome/rob-2-0-tool).
- **ROBINS-I:** the original 2016 tool remains available. **ROBINS-I V2, 20 November 2025, is explicitly a draft subject to change** and currently describes follow-up/cohort studies of interventions. Pin the chosen version and result-level estimand in the protocol. [ROBINS-I V2](https://www.riskofbias.info/welcome/robins-i-v2). Cochrane directs RCT/quasi-RCT reviews to RoB 2 and non-randomized intervention reviews to ROBINS-I. [Cochrane guidance](https://www.cochrane.org/ms/learn/courses-and-resources/cochrane-methodology/risk-bias/about-risk-bias-2-rob-2).
- **NOS:** it is a star-based quality-assessment scale for cohort and case-control studies, organized around selection, comparability, and exposure/outcome ascertainment. Do not convert a star total into a RoB judgment, use it as a generic tool for all observational designs, or feed a score mechanically into GRADE. [NOS owner documentation](https://ohri.ca/en/who-we-are/core-facilities-and-platforms/ottawa-methods-centre/newcastle-ottawa-scale).
- **GRADE:** rate certainty per critical/important outcome and explicitly consider risk of bias, imprecision, inconsistency, indirectness, publication bias, and applicable upgrading domains. Produce evidence profiles or Summary of Findings tables. [GRADE requirements](https://www.gradeworkinggroup.org/).
- **SWiM:** use when effect estimates cannot appropriately be meta-analysed. It requires an explicit synthesis structure, metric, method, grouping/prioritisation, and heterogeneity investigation. It is a reporting guideline and is not a license for unstructured narrative synthesis. [SWiM statement](https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.l6890).

## Software evidence and recommended boundary

- `meta::metagen()` is appropriate for generic inverse-variance estimates and standard errors. It supports a `cluster` and `rho` path for three-level analysis, plus explicit random-effects CI and prediction-interval controls. The current helper uses only the basic, unclustered path. [Current `metagen` manual](https://rdrr.io/cran/meta/man/metagen.html).
- `metafor::rma.uni()` is suitable for standard univariate models. Use `rma.mv()` when effects have known or assumed dependence and preserve the variance-covariance matrix. `metafor::regtest()` provides a funnel-asymmetry regression test, but its availability is not an indication that the test is applicable. [metafor reference](https://wviechtb.github.io/metafor/reference/index.html).
- `statsmodels` is useful for transparent Python checks of precomputed independent effects. Its `combine_effects()` supports `iterated`/Paule-Mandel and DL, and exposes fixed-scale and estimated-scale/HKSJ-style results, but it is not the primary engine for multilevel, multivariate, rare-event, or full-review synthesis. [statsmodels API](https://www.statsmodels.org/dev/generated/statsmodels.stats.meta_analysis.combine_effects.html).

## Compact self-contained skill design

Do not turn `ce-effect-size` into a full review skill. Keep its documented power-anchor boundary. Add one new, self-contained skill directory, for example `skills/ce-systematic-review-meta-analysis/`, with these local references:

1. `references/protocol-and-estimand.md`: PICO/PECO, study family, target effect, outcome definition, time point, population, contrast, eligibility, hierarchy for multiple reports/results, registration status, amendment ledger, and an explicit no-pool option.
2. `references/effect-data-contract.md`: a result-level schema with study/report/result IDs, arm-level data where available, effect/variance provenance, adjustment, RoB result linkage, dependence group, covariance source/assumption, and exclusion reason. Validate before any model runs.
3. `references/appraisal-certainty-missing-evidence.md`: current tool routing and versions, dual-review/consensus fields, NOS boundary, ROB-ME, GRADE per outcome, and Summary of Findings requirements.
4. `references/synthesis-decision.md`: compatible-synthesis criteria; fixed/common versus random effects are prespecified analyses, not selected from a heterogeneity test; rare-event and zero-event routes; SWiM route with an explicit structured method; subgroup/meta-regression multiplicity and minimum-information cautions.
5. `references/r-and-python.md`: named, version-pinned API calls and limitations. R is primary. Python is a secondary independent-effect check unless the review specification demonstrably fits its supported API.
6. `references/diagnostics.md`: influence/leave-one-out, tau-squared/CI, HKSJ policy, PI policy, reporting-bias/ROB-ME, funnel/Egger gates, and language that does not equate asymmetry with publication bias.

Every reference remains inside that skill directory, as required by this repository's portable-skill rule.

## Synthetic validation scenarios

No package installation or runtime validation was performed in this audit. Use only synthetic aggregate examples and assert decisions as well as numbers.

| Scenario | Required assertion |
|---|---|
| Eight independent log risk ratios with variances | R `meta` and `metafor` agree within tolerance on the declared REML point estimate. The run records HKSJ/CI and PI policy, tau-squared, I-squared, and package versions. |
| Three studies, each reporting two correlated outcomes | The workflow blocks naïve row-wise pooling. It either selects one protocol-ranked result or requires a covariance assumption and uses `rma.mv()`/three-level modeling. |
| Two-arm sparse binary data with single-zero and double-zero studies | The workflow refuses the generic CI parser, requires arm events/denominators, logs the zero-event rule, and runs predeclared rare-event sensitivity analyses. |
| Mixed crude ORs, adjusted ORs with different covariate sets, and different follow-up times | The workflow stops before pooling and reports the estimand conflict. It must not silently normalize labels and pool. |
| Eight studies with apparent asymmetry | No Egger/regression test is run. The report states that `k < 10` and that asymmetry is not diagnostic, then records a qualitative missing-evidence assessment. |
| Twelve studies with plausible SE/effect relationship and no major unexplained heterogeneity | A funnel plot and a prespecified asymmetry test can run, but the conclusion must say "small-study effects/funnel asymmetry", not "publication bias proven". |
| Heterogeneous estimates not suitable for quantitative pooling | The workflow invokes a structured SWiM path with grouped evidence, a stated metric/synthesis rule, and no fabricated pooled effect. |
| Python independent-effect parity check | `statsmodels.combine_effects(..., method_re="pm")` is compared with an R Paule-Mandel analysis, not called REML. The expected HKSJ-scale output and the API limitation are asserted. |

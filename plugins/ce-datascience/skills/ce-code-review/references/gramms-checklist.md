Good Reporting of A Mixed Methods Study (GRAMMS).
6-item checklist for reporting studies that combine qualitative and quantitative methods.

**Primary reference:** O'Cathain A, Murphy E, Nicholl J. The quality of mixed methods studies in health services research. J Health Serv Res Policy. 2008;13(2):92-98. doi:10.1258/jhsrp.2007.007074. PMID: 18416914.

## Justification

### Item 1 -- Justification

**Description:** Describe why a mixed methods approach was used to address the research question.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Explicit statement of why neither qualitative nor quantitative methods alone were sufficient
- Mixed methods purpose articulated (triangulation, complementarity, development, initiation, expansion)

---

## Design

### Item 2 -- Design

**Description:** Describe the design in terms of its purpose, the priority given to each method, and the sequence of the methods.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Named design (e.g., convergent, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential) and its purpose
- Relative priority of the qualitative and quantitative strands stated
- Sequence or timing of the strands (concurrent or sequential, with order)
- Optional design diagram or notation (e.g., QUAL + quan, QUAN -> qual)

---

## Methods

### Item 3 -- Description of each method

**Description:** Describe each method in terms of its sampling, data collection, and analysis.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Sampling strategy and sample size for each strand (purposive, random, theoretical; saturation or power)
- Data collection for each strand (survey instrument, administrative extraction, interviews, focus groups, observation)
- Analysis method for each strand (regression, survival analysis, SPC; thematic, framework, grounded theory) and software

---

## Integration

### Item 4 -- Integration

**Description:** Describe where in the study integration occurred, how it was done, and who took part in it.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Stage(s) of integration named (sampling, data collection, analysis, interpretation)
- Integration technique described (joint display, case-ordered matrix, data transformation, following a thread)
- Team members who carried out the integration identified

---

## Limitations

### Item 5 -- Limitations arising from mixing

**Description:** Describe any limitation of one method that arose because the other method was also used.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Limitations specific to the combination (e.g., interviews sampled from survey respondents inheriting non-response bias; a qualitative phase constrained by quantitative timelines), not only generic limitations of each method

---

## Insights

### Item 6 -- Insights from mixing

**Description:** Describe any insights gained from mixing or integrating the methods.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Meta-inferences stated separately from single-strand conclusions
- Convergent, complementary, and divergent findings across strands identified and explained
- What the combination revealed that neither strand showed alone
